Section one

What we found.

Everything below was read off mi-gas.net on 10 August 2026 and checked again on 18 August. Every one of them is checkable in about a minute, and the links go to your own site rather than to a screenshot of it. If something has changed since, it has changed and we are wrong.

There is no email address anywhere on the site

Not on the contact page, not in the footer, not on a product page. The only two routes are an Instagram DM and a Patreon link, and neither is a business inbox. That means a licensed operator wanting facility work, a grower asking whether a guide covers coco, and a bot all land in the same inbox with nothing to sort them by, no record, and no way to reply from anywhere except your phone.

Open your contact page ↗

All six of your reviews are image files

R1.png through R6.png. Google cannot read a word of them, so they do nothing for you in search. None of the six carries alt text, so a screen reader announces nothing at all. The one asset a consultant genuinely cannot manufacture is invisible to everybody except a sighted visitor who happened to scroll to the right page. Transcribing them is about an hour of somebody's time and it is the highest-return hour on this whole project.

Try to select the text on your reviews page ↗

Three of your five page titles are unedited platform defaults

The guides page is titled Store 1 — MI Gas. That is the string Squarespace generates when nobody writes one, and it is what shows in a search result, in a browser tab and in a shared link. Your reviews page is Reviews 1 and your contact page is Contact 3. Your home page is just MI Gas, so of the five, only the merch store says what it is. The page selling your programs is introducing itself as Store 1.

Open it and look at the browser tab ↗

Every one of the seven store items is sold out, and you find out one at a time

Three hoodies at $41.40, three tees at $31.90, one camo trucker at $50. A visitor clicks through the grid discovering it seven separate times. A storefront whose only message is "sold out" costs a little trust on every visit, and it collects nothing in exchange. Said once at the top with a restock list, the same dead page becomes a mailing list.

Count them yourself ↗

The Complete Package is a fifth tile among four cheaper ones

It is $500 against $600 for the same three guides at $200 each, which makes it both your highest-value product and your best offer. On the page it is the same size tile as everything else, in the same row, so the most valuable thing you sell looks like one more option rather than the answer.

Compare the five tiles ↗

There are roughly twice as many product URLs as products

Old and duplicate addresses left behind by the platform. Some of them are what a search engine has indexed, which means some share of the people looking for your guides are landing on a URL you are not maintaining.

No structured data anywhere on the site

Nothing tells Google that this is a professional service, what it costs, where it operates or what it sells. That is the difference between a search result that is a blue link and one that carries your service, your price range and your location. It is invisible to you and it is free to add.

Section two

The part that matters most.

You already wrote the thesis of this proposal

In one of your own posts, about educators who charge for information:

"… I don't see them backed up with the work! They usually feature some random up close up nug shots, shots of gardens with somewhat questionable health and graphs and words. It's hard to even tell if they are their own pictures as there are no distinguishing features that let you know it's legit. You can see the same rooms every time in our shots, over and over again with new plants and the same consistent success and quality."

That is exactly right, and it is the whole argument for rebuilding this site. You are backed up by the work. You publish the same rooms run after run. You run tissue analysis on your own crop and post the results. You moved the program to lower EC and your average THC per strain went up 25 to 30%. You say plainly when you do not know something, which is the rarest thing in this industry and the reason people pay you.

None of that is on your website. The six reviews are pictures. The rooms are on Instagram. The programs are behind a page called Store 1. The only way to hire you is a DM. A grower who has never heard of you arrives at mi-gas.net and sees a tidy storefront with everything sold out.

The gap is not between you and better growers. It is between what you have proven and what a stranger with a budget can see in the ninety seconds before they leave.

The booking, which is the thing you actually asked for

You said you wanted something like Calendly with the whole experience made better. A scheduler sells you a calendar. The calendar was never the problem: the problem is that the first twenty minutes of a paid call go on questions a form could have asked.

Eight questions before the callStage by day, canopy, media, feed, lights, nutrient line, runoff volume with EC and pH, and what they want out of the hour. Your questions, in lib/site.ts, editable without touching a component.
Photos off a phone, which normally failA canopy photo is 3 to 8 MB and the request cap is about 4.5 MB, so four straight off a camera roll physically cannot be posted. The browser resizes each one to 1600px on its long edge and re-encodes it before it leaves the device, and renames it so your inbox is not four copies of IMG_4821.jpg.
A real calendar inviteAn .ics to both of you with a 30-minute alarm, so your own calendar becomes the record of what is booked.
Honest slot lengthsA 90-minute consult is never offered at 11:30 in a window that closes at noon.
One timezone, labelledEastern, and it says so. A call missed by an hour costs more than a booking.
Availability you edit yourselfAdded after the call, because you asked for it: a PIN-gated page where you set your weekly hours, days off and lead time from your phone. No commit, no waiting on us.
Your calendar closes booked slotsAlso from the call: point the site at your own calendar's private feed and every event on it removes the times it covers. A confirmed booking lands on your calendar via the invite, the feed carries it, the slot is gone for the next visitor.
The visitor who will not do a formName and a number, and we call them. Some people know exactly what they need; the site now captures the ones who just want to talk first.

New since the call: co-management

The offer you outlined on August 27 has its own page, in your own terms: irrigation and environment steered remotely off the room's sensors, scheduling planned with their labor, $50 to $150 per light by project size, the sensor requirement stated plainly (Aroya, Pinnacle, Growlink or equivalent, no TrolMaster), and the head-grower condition before the ask. With it: an application that collects the room (light count and sensors first, because those price it), a printable one-pager built to be handed to a partner, and the page's own link card for when it gets forwarded. The project minimum and the billing cadence are the two numbers it still needs from you.
